# Planning

Use iterative SDLC: define the customer problem, analyse requirements, design journeys and data, implement a small vertical workflow, then test and present evidence. Preserve existing working code and record scope changes.

Proposed project responsibilities cover product coordination, business analysis, interface design, development and quality/presentation. These are functions, not an allocation to invented team members. They are not displayed as staff roles on customer pages.

## Feasibility

- Operational: validate a limited service zone, safe travel and realistic technician workload. No target should encourage unsafe travel.
- Technical: portable pressing/steam care is the intended model; future washing technology needs independent trials. Static hosting plus localStorage supports a classroom demo only.
- Financial: fees and a 60% illustrative care-subtotal technician share are assumptions. Model wages, equipment, transport, consumables, insurance and customer acquisition before claiming profitability.
- Human factors: readable forms, clear treatment consent and honest simulation labels support trust.
- Legal/environmental: obtain local advice on permits, worker status, consumer rights, insurance, privacy and measurable environmental claims before launch.

## Porter's Five Forces

New entrants: medium; equipment is accessible, but trusted training and route density take work. Suppliers: low-to-medium; equipment alternatives exist but skilled capacity may constrain growth. Buyers: medium; switching is easy, so clear pricing matters. Substitutes: high; home ironing, steamers and cleaners solve adjacent needs. Rivalry: medium; focus on urgent on-site suitability rather than copying collection platforms.

## Risks and delivery

Prioritise one reliable customer-to-technician journey over breadth. Mitigate unsuitable treatment through assessment/referral, unclear pricing through review, data loss through explicit save errors, and overclaiming through visible demo limits. Original rubric verification and supervised usability testing remain tasks for the assessment team.
